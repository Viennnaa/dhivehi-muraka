Dhivehi Muraka is an original Thaana typeface for the Dhivehi language of the Maldives. It covers the complete Unicode Thaana block: the 24 base consonants, naviyani, the 14 Arabic transliteration letters, and all 11 fili.

Muraka means coral. The Maldives is built from it, and this typeface was built the same way, point by point, over several days of drawing.

Design

The letterforms take the calligraphic skeleton of Thaana and steady it for the screen. Counters are opened, stroke weight is evened out, and the posture is flatter than the reed-pen tradition the script descends from. It is meant for interfaces, government publications and body text, and holds its shape down to 12 px, where Thaana is hardest to set.

Vowel marks

Fili are not drawn onto the consonants. They are separate zero-width marks carrying OpenType anchors, so each one centres itself on whatever consonant it follows — the narrow letters and the wide ones alike, without a special case for either.

Provenance

Every Thaana outline was drawn by hand. No existing font was opened, imported, traced or used as a source of coordinates. An existing Thaana face was displayed on screen as a proportion reference while drawing, the way a printed specimen would be, and the build verifies on every run that no glyph shares coordinates with it.

The Latin comes from Inter by Rasmus Andersson, merged under the SIL Open Font License and credited in the font's name table.
